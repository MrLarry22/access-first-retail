Concept Prototype

Smart Fitting Room Assistant

Reimagining the fitting room experience with simple technology that removes friction for every customer.

💡 The Problem

Customers discover a garment doesn't fit, then must redress, leave, find the right size, and return — or abandon the purchase. For customers with mobility challenges, this friction is a dealbreaker.

✨ The Solution

📺

Wall Display

Tap to request a different size, colour, or style without leaving the room

📡

Live Inventory

Real-time stock check shows available sizes and alternatives instantly

🔔

Staff Alerts

Team member receives instant notification to deliver the requested item

👕

Smart Pairing

AI suggests complementary LifeWear pieces based on the customer's selection

📊

Data Insights

Anonymised session data informs inventory planning and demand forecasting

♿

Universal Access

Adjustable mirror heights, voice control option, and seated-height interface

Framework

7 Principles of Universal Design — Applied to Retail

How each timeless design principle translates to better shopping experiences.

1

Equitable Use

Useful and marketable to people with diverse abilities.

In Retail: Online and in-store experiences offering equivalent product access, pricing, and service quality for all customers.
2

Flexibility in Use

Accommodates a wide range of individual preferences and abilities.

In Retail: Multiple checkout options — self-service kiosks, staffed counters, mobile payment, and assisted checkout for those who need it.
3

Simple & Intuitive

Easy to understand, regardless of experience, knowledge, or language.

In Retail: Clear store layout with visual category groupings, pictogram signage, and intuitive size labelling across global markets.
4

Perceptible Information

Communicates necessary information effectively to the user.

In Retail: Price tags with large font, tactile fabric labels, and digital screens showing product details in multiple languages.
5

Tolerance for Error

Minimises hazards and adverse consequences of accidental actions.

In Retail: Generous return policies, easy-to-undo online cart changes, and fitting room mirrors placed to prevent accidental damage.
6

Low Physical Effort

Can be used efficiently and comfortably with minimum fatigue.

In Retail: Products displayed at reachable heights, baskets available on every floor, and seating areas throughout the store.
7

Size & Space for Approach

Appropriate size and space provided for approach, reach, and use.

In Retail: Wide aisles accommodating wheelchairs and strollers, spacious fitting rooms, and accessible countertop heights.

About the Creator

Paul Lawrence is a final-year Computer Science student at Dedan Kimathi University of Technology — and the first person in his family and village to attend university.

Living with contractures, a physical disability affecting joint mobility, Paul learned early that every barrier is a design problem waiting to be solved. This mindset drives his commitment to building technology that serves everyone, not just the majority.

As an Equity Leaders Program scholar (selected among Kenya's top KCSE students nationally), a peer tutor who has supported 30+ students in learning Python, and a community tech mentor who teaches digital literacy to youth in his home village, Paul bridges the gap between technology and human impact.

His academic work centres on accessible web development — from WCAG 2.1-aligned portfolios to full-stack platforms with built-in accessibility features. He believes that the future of retail belongs to companies that design for the full spectrum of human diversity.
